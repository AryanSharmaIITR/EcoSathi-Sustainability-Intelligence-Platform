# E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 — Government of India

**Authority:** Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC)
**Enacted:** November 2022
**Supersedes:** E-Waste Management Rules, 2016

---

## Scope — What Counts as E-Waste
E-waste means electrical and electronic equipment (EEE) that has been discarded, including:
- IT & Telecom equipment: computers, laptops, mobile phones, servers, printers
- Consumer electronics: TVs, audio/video equipment, cameras
- Large household appliances: refrigerators, air conditioners, washing machines
- Small household appliances: vacuum cleaners, microwaves, irons
- Lighting equipment: fluorescent lamps, LEDs (at end-of-life)
- Medical devices (non-implantable)
- Industrial equipment with electronic components

---

## Key Provisions

### Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)
- Producers of EEE must register on the **EPR Portal** (cpcbewaste.nic.in).
- Annual EPR targets set by CPCB — producers must collect and channel a percentage of EEE sold in previous years.
- EPR target (2023–24 onwards): 60% of EEE placed on market in base year; ramping to 80% by 2026–27.
- Producers may fulfil EPR via:
  1. Their own collection infrastructure.
  2. PRO (Producer Responsibility Organisation) registered with CPCB.
  3. Buying EPR certificates from registered recyclers.

### Rule 11 — Duties of Consumers / Bulk Consumers
- **Bulk consumers** (institutions, companies) must:
  - Segregate e-waste at source.
  - Handover e-waste **only** to authorised dismantlers / recyclers (listed on CPCB portal).
  - Maintain records of e-waste handed over (manifests) for at least 3 years.
  - Not dispose of e-waste with municipal solid waste.

### Rule 16 — Authorised Dismantlers / Recyclers
- Must hold valid authorisation from State Pollution Control Board (SPCB) or Pollution Control Committee (PCC).
- Only accept e-waste from producers, consumers, collection centres, PROs.
- Must maintain records and submit annual returns to CPCB.

## Hazardous Substances in E-Waste
E-waste contains hazardous materials requiring controlled handling:
| Substance | Found In | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Lead (Pb) | CRT screens, solder | Neurological damage, soil contamination |
| Mercury (Hg) | LCD backlights, thermostats | Highly toxic, bio-accumulates |
| Cadmium (Cd) | Batteries, semiconductors | Carcinogenic, kidney damage |
| Hexavalent Chromium | Metal housings, cables | Carcinogenic |
| Brominated Flame Retardants | PCBs, plastics | Persistent organic pollutants |

## Recycling SOP — E-Waste Handling at Institutional Level

1. **Identification:** Tag and record asset (serial number, type, approx. weight).
2. **Segregation:** Store e-waste in a designated, locked, dry collection area — do NOT mix with other waste.
3. **Data destruction:** Wipe or physically destroy storage media before handover.
4. **Contact authorised recycler:** Verify current CPCB / SPCB authorisation (check cpcbewaste.nic.in).
5. **Generate manifest:** Fill e-waste transfer manifest (Form 3 under E-Waste Rules 2022) before every consignment.
6. **Handover:** Physical transfer with signed manifest; recycler returns counter-signed copy.
7. **Record keeping:** Retain manifests and receipts for minimum 3 years; submit annual return to SPCB.
8. **EPR compliance:** If the institution is also a producer, file EPR returns on CPCB portal by 30 June each year.

---

## Penalties
- Disposal of e-waste in municipal bins or open areas: Environmental Compensation under EPR rules.
- Failure to hand over to authorised recycler: Fine up to ₹1 lakh + remediation cost.
- Fake EPR certificates: Criminal prosecution under Environment Protection Act, 1986.
